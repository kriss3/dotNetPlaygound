From doFactory.com

The Observer design pattern defines a one-to-many dependency between objects so that when one object changes state, 
all its dependents are notified and updated automatically. 

From Christopher Okhravi:
It is a move from Poll architecture to Push architecture.

Classic approach is Poll => The client is constantly asking the "Producer" of the data: 
    - Have you changed? 
    - Have you changed your state?

Observer pattern help to move to Push paradigm:
The "Producer" of the data NOW is responsible for "NOTIFYING" ALL subscribers there is a "CHANGE" and 
subscribers will now receive a new state of the published data.
    - I have change, now do what you need to do with the new data I'm providing.

The nature of the "Producer" of the data is, it 'KNOWS' about subscribers.
Subscribers need to be 'REGISTERED' with the 'PRODUCER'.

NOW Terms:
    - 'PRODUCER' of the data is Observable
    - 'CONSUMERS' of the data (subscribers) are Observers (one or many)

A lot of times online and in books: Observable or the PRODUCER is called: the Subject.

Details: 
IObservable ---> (0..*)IObserver (IObservable HAS 0 or many IObservers)


ConcreteObservable (Implements IObservable)

ConcreteObserver (Implements IObserver)

ALSO (Important):
ConceteObserver has an open arrow to ConcreteObservable.
Through ctor, ConcreteObserver has access to ConcreteObservable.
It looks like bi-directional communication between ConcreteObserver and ConcreteObservable.
